# Data log

Generated by `scripts/06_data_log.py` from the data files. Do not edit by hand; re-run the script.

## How to read this

Every data point carries a `source` and a `confidence`:

| Confidence | Meaning |
|---|---|
| documented | A census or official count, a figure recorded at the time, a surveyed height, a securely dated event, or (for footprints) a GHSL epoch observed from satellite imagery. |
| estimated | A published scholarly or institutional estimate (Chandler, Modelski, a historian, the UN), or the GHSL 2025 epoch, which is a projection. |
| reconstructed | Our own interpolation or model. Always labelled on screen. |

## Methods that apply to every city

**Ranking and extent.** The 50 cities are the top 50 of UN World Urbanization Prospects 2025 by 2025
population. Each city's extent is its urban centre polygon in GHS-UCDB R2024A (the polygon containing the
city centre). Guangzhou and Shenzhen share one GHSL urban centre and are split by weighted distance (see
`docs/DECISIONS.md`).

**Population.** Points before 1950 were researched city by city (sources in each file). 1950-1970 are
UN WUP 2018 urban-agglomeration estimates; 1975-2025 are UN WUP 2025 urban-extent estimates; 2026 is a
linear interpolation between the UN's 2025 and 2030 values. These are three different definitions of
"the city" and the series is not rescaled to hide the joins. Between points the app interpolates
log-linearly and labels the result; before the first sourced point it shows no number.

**Footprint 1975-2025 (GHSL).** GHS-BUILT-S R2023A, 100 m grid, epochs every five years. A cell is
built-up when at least 5 % of it (500 m2) is built surface. 1975-2020 are satellite-derived
(`documented`); 2025 is a GHSL projection (`estimated`). No tile download failed and no resolution
fallback was needed for any city.

**Footprint before 1975 (reconstructed).** There is no global built-up map before 1975. Following the
spec, the 1975 footprint is contracted backward toward the historic core:

1. Every 1975 built-up cell is ranked by least-cost distance from the historic core. Crossing densely
   built cells is cheap (dense is assumed old), sparse cells cost more, and unbuilt gaps such as water
   cost most, so the reconstructed city grows along built corridors rather than across bays. Strong
   secondary density peaks at least 6 km from the core are treated as satellite towns that begin to
   grow once the main front is half-way to them.
2. The target area for year Y is `A(1975) x P(Y)/P(1975) / m(Y)`, where P is the population series
   (researched points, then UN WUP 2018 to 1975) and m(Y) is how much denser cities were than in 1975:
   density is assumed to fall 1.5 % a year from 1890 to 1975 and to be constant before 1890, after
   Angel, Parent, Civco & Blei (2010), capped at 500 people per built-up hectare.
3. The footprint at year Y is the A(Y) lowest-ranked cells. It never shrinks: population losses show in
   the counter, not as demolition. Before the first sourced population point the area ramps up from a
   single cell at the start year.

No dated historical maps were used as anchors (none are available in a form the pipeline can read), so
the pre-1975 shape is a model of where a city of that size most plausibly stood, not a record. The
Atlas of Urban Expansion cross-check is reported under "Cross-checks" where it was done.

**Buildings.** Generated, not surveyed. Each building's year is the year its cell enters the footprint
plus jitter; present-day heights are calibrated to GHS-BUILT-H R2023A (average net building height per
cell, 2018); earlier generations are capped by era (10 m before 1700 rising to 30 m by 1900, 60 m by
1930, 110 m by 1960) and replaced later by taller ones. Detail follows built volume: the core is
resolved to 100 m cells and quarter-cell lots, the periphery is merged into 200 m and 400 m blocks.
The RNG is seeded per city, so rebuilds are identical.

**Landmarks.** Real position, height and completion year from the cited source. The 3D shape is
schematic.

Research notes: Start date: the site at the mouth of the Ciliwung was the Sunda Kingdom's port of Kalapa (Sunda Kelapa) before the events usually given as the city's founding. The earliest securely dated document that describes the port town itself is Tomé Pires's Suma Oriental (written 1512-1515; Portuguese ships called in 1513), so 1513 is used as the earliest documented settlement. The port is certainly older, but no dated source for its founding was found; the 13th-century Chinese Zhu Fan Zhi describes a harbour of Sunda that is not securely identified with Kalapa, and the mid-5th-century Tugu inscription found in North Jakarta documents river works in the area, not a town. The city's official anniversary is 22 June 1527 (conquest of Kalapa, traditionally the renaming to Jayakarta, although surviving records do not confirm who led it or the renaming), and the colonial city of Batavia dates from 1619. The start date is therefore contested between 1513 (used here), 1527 and 1619. Population: no figure was found for Kalapa or Jayakarta, so the series starts in 1673; the app should show no reliable estimate from 1513 to 1673. Figures for 1673-1850 are Chandler's estimates for Batavia. From 1880 the figures are colonial counts as reported in secondary sources, and they do not share one boundary: the 1920 figure (306,000) and the 1930 figure (533,000) are larger than the municipal totals quoted elsewhere (253,800 and 435,000), which suggests they include the adjoining Meester Cornelis area that was merged into Batavia in 1935, but the pages consulted do not say so explicitly. The original 1920 and 1930 census volumes were not consulted. Forbes (2004) also gives 823,000 for 1948, which conflicts with 847,483 for 1945 (Twang) and 1,340,625 for 1949 (Merrillees); it was left out. Chandler's 1893 (110,000), 1900 (115,000) and 1925 (290,000) estimates were left out in favour of the counts nearby. Landmarks: no sourced height was found for the pre-1800 buildings of Old Batavia (the 1710 Stadhuis, now the Jakarta History Museum; Gereja Sion, 1695; Kota Intan Bridge, 1628), for Hotel Indonesia or for Gelora Bung Karno Stadium, so they are omitted; the oldest landmark with a sourced height is the 1839 harbour tower. The Selamat Datang and Dirgantara monuments were omitted because the sources give inconsistent or partial heights. Skyscraper heights and years are from the CTBUH Skyscraper Center.

Population 1872-1945 is the official Tokyo Metropolitan Government series for Tokyo Prefecture (later Metropolis), not the city proper; the prefecture was enlarged in the 1890s. Landmarks: the Edo Castle keep height (51 m) and the Sensō-ji pagoda height (podium plus tower) come from Wikipedia rather than a survey, and are marked estimated; coordinates for the Zōjō-ji gate and the Sensō-ji pagoda are those of the temple compounds, accurate to roughly 100 m. Landmarks: heights for the Red Fort, Purana Qila and Tughlaqabad are wall heights; the Rashtrapati Bhavan height has no primary citation. Left out for lack of a sourced height or position: Safdarjung's Tomb, Jantar Mantar, the Parliament buildings, and the Civic Centre (112 m, 2010; listed by the CTBUH Skyscraper Center without coordinates). The CTBUH database lists almost nothing else for Delhi proper; the tall towers of the region are in Noida and Gurgaon, outside the city.
